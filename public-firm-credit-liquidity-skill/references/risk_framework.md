## 1. Receivables Quality

Purpose: assess whether revenue is converting into collectable receivables and whether collection risk is rising.

Review:

- Accounts receivable trend
- Revenue growth versus AR growth
- AR as a percentage of revenue
- DSO, if calculable
- Aging schedule, if disclosed
- Customer concentration, if disclosed
- Factoring, securitization, or receivable sale disclosures

Potential red flags:

- AR grows materially faster than revenue
- DSO increases meaningfully
- Aging deteriorates or past-due balances rise
- Receivable concentration increases
- Revenue growth depends on extended payment terms

## 2. Bad Debt / Credit Loss Risk

Purpose: assess whether expected customer credit losses are rising and whether reserves appear adequate.

Review:

- Allowance for doubtful accounts or expected credit loss allowance
- Allowance as a percentage of gross receivables
- Bad debt expense / impairment losses
- Write-offs
- Provision movements
- Changes in credit-loss methodology or assumptions
- Management commentary on customer credit risk

Potential red flags:

- Bad debt expense rises faster than revenue
- Write-offs increase while allowance coverage falls
- Allowance ratio declines despite worsening receivable trends
- Management notes weaker customer credit quality

## 3. Short-Term Liquidity

Purpose: assess whether the company can meet near-term obligations.

Review:

- Cash and cash equivalents
- Restricted cash
- Current assets
- Current liabilities
- Short-term borrowings
- Current portion of long-term debt
- Current ratio
- Quick ratio
- Working capital
- Available revolving credit facilities, if disclosed
- MD&A liquidity discussion

Potential red flags:

- Cash declines while current liabilities rise
- Current ratio or quick ratio deteriorates
- Restricted cash is a large part of reported cash
- Material short-term debt exceeds visible liquidity
- Management discloses liquidity stress or dependence on external financing

## 4. Cash Flow Conversion

Purpose: assess whether accounting earnings convert into operating cash flow.

Review:

- Net income versus cash flow from operations
- CFO margin
- Free cash flow, if calculable
- Working-capital adjustments in the cash flow statement
- Repeated gap between earnings and CFO

Potential red flags:

- Positive earnings with weak or negative CFO
- CFO deterioration explained by receivables build-up
- Free cash flow turns negative due to operating weakness rather than growth capex alone

## 5. Working Capital Pressure

Purpose: detect whether operating growth is being funded by stretched working capital.

Review:

- Changes in receivables
- Changes in inventory
- Changes in payables
- Working capital change
- Cash conversion cycle, if calculable

Potential red flags:

- AR and inventory both rise faster than revenue
- Payables are stretched to preserve cash
- Working capital consumes cash for multiple periods
- Inventory build suggests slowing demand or channel stuffing risk

## 6. Near-Term Debt / Refinancing Pressure

Purpose: identify whether debt maturities, interest cost, or refinancing dependence could create near-term stress.

Review:

- Short-term borrowings
- Current portion of long-term debt
- Debt maturity schedule
- Interest expense trend
- Floating-rate exposure, if disclosed
- Available credit lines and undrawn capacity
- Covenant or refinancing commentary

Potential red flags:

- Large maturities within 12-24 months
- Rising interest expense with weaker cash flow
- Heavy reliance on refinancing
- Covenant pressure or waiver language
- Credit facility availability declines

## Interpreting Signals

Avoid mechanical conclusions. A red flag should be framed as a signal requiring explanation, not proof of distress.

In blind reviews, do not infer the company type or expected risk pattern from the selection process. Let the most material public evidence determine the primary risk drivers.

When possible, provide:

- Observed fact
- Why it matters
- Possible benign explanation
- Possible adverse explanation
- Follow-up question
