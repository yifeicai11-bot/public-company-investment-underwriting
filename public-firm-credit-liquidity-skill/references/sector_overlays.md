## How To Use Overlays

1. Run the core public-company analysis first.
2. Identify the primary firm type from filings, segment disclosure, revenue model, and SIC/industry description.
3. Apply only the relevant overlay points.
4. Do not excuse a weak metric solely because it is common in the sector.
5. Explain whether a signal is normal for the business model, more severe than expected, or unresolved due to missing disclosure.

Use overlays to improve:

- Interpretation of AR, inventory, deferred revenue, contract assets, CFO, capex, debt, and liquidity.
- Follow-up questions.
- Confidence level when public data lacks sector-specific detail.

## SaaS / Software

Common features:

- Subscription billing and annual upfront invoicing can make AR, deferred revenue, and CFO seasonal.
- Deferred revenue / contract liabilities may support cash flow.
- Stock-based compensation can widen the gap between net income and CFO.
- Debt may include convertibles, capped calls, or repurchase activity.

Interpretation adjustments:

- AR / quarterly revenue can look high after billing cycles; compare AR trend with billings, deferred revenue, and collection commentary when available.
- Positive CFO despite GAAP net loss may be normal if deferred revenue and non-cash expenses are large.
- Low cash coverage of current liabilities may be less concerning if current liabilities include deferred revenue rather than cash obligations.
- Convertible debt needs maturity, conversion, cash settlement, and repurchase detail.

Follow-up focus:

- Deferred revenue trend and billings quality.
- Gross retention / churn if disclosed.
- AR aging and customer concentration.
- Convertible debt maturity and settlement terms.
- Cash flow excluding working-capital timing and stock-based compensation context.

## Retail

Common features:

- Inventory, markdown risk, leases, and seasonality are central.
- AR may be immaterial for cash/card-heavy models.
- Payables can move with inventory buying cycles.
- Lease obligations can matter even when debt is low.

Interpretation adjustments:

- Receivables may be Low risk if AR is immaterial.
- Inventory growth is not automatically negative, but it requires seasonality, sales trend, and markdown context.
- Current ratio may look healthy because inventory is high; assess inventory quality.
- No financial debt does not mean no fixed obligations; check lease commitments.

Follow-up focus:

- Inventory aging, turnover, markdowns, and post-quarter sales.
- Same-store sales or comparable sales if disclosed.
- Lease commitments and rent obligations.
- Supplier/payables terms.

## Wholesale / Distribution

Common features:

- Receivables, inventory, payables, supplier rebates, and vendor terms are central to liquidity.
- Reported margins may be thin, so working-capital timing can have a large effect on operating cash flow.
- Debt and revolver usage can support seasonal inventory and acquisition activity.
- Cash balances can look low relative to current liabilities if the model relies on fast inventory turnover and supplier financing.

Interpretation adjustments:

- Low cash / current liabilities is not automatically severe if inventory turns quickly and borrowing availability is strong, but it requires revolver and supplier-term evidence.
- AR and inventory should be read together; rising AR plus rising inventory can indicate demand slowdown, customer collection pressure, or expansion.
- Positive CFO can be driven by working-capital release, so compare it with net income, capex, and debt service.
- Revenue tag gaps or unusual fiscal calendars should be noted rather than ignored.

Follow-up focus:

- Inventory turnover, obsolescence, and gross margin trend.
- Customer concentration, AR aging, and bad debt experience.
- Supplier terms, payables stretch, and vendor rebates.
- Revolver capacity, borrowing base, covenant headroom, and acquisition-related debt.

## Manufacturing / Industrial

Common features:

- AR and inventory are usually material.
- Customer concentration and payment terms can drive liquidity.
- Capex and raw-material cycles affect free cash flow.
- Inventory build may reflect demand, input costs, production timing, or slowing orders.

Interpretation adjustments:

- AR plus inventory rising while CFO weakens is a stronger warning signal than either item alone.
- A low allowance ratio requires customer-quality and aging support.
- Current ratio can look strong while cash is tied up in AR/inventory.
- Free cash flow should be separated into operating weakness versus planned capex.

Follow-up focus:

- AR aging, customer concentration, and extended payment terms.
- Inventory composition, obsolescence, and backlog/order trend.
- Supplier terms and payables stretch.
- Capex plan and maintenance vs growth capex.

## Contractor / Government Services

Common features:

- Contract assets, unbilled receivables, billed receivables, and project timing matter.
- AR can be high relative to quarterly revenue because billing and milestone timing are uneven.
- Customer credit quality may be strong, but collection timing can still pressure cash.
- Backlog and funded backlog can affect revenue visibility.

Interpretation adjustments:

- High AR is not automatically poor quality if tied to government or investment-grade customers, but unbilled balances and aging matter.
- Negative quarterly CFO can be timing-related; compare with annual CFO and MD&A explanation.
- Low allowance may be reasonable for high-quality customers, but concentration and dispute risk should be checked.
- Debt risk should be assessed against backlog, CFO, and revolver availability.

Follow-up focus:

- Billed vs unbilled receivables / contract assets.
- Customer concentration and government exposure.
- Contract disputes, claims, or delayed milestones.
- Backlog conversion and working-capital timing.

## Biotech / Pre-Revenue or Low-Revenue R&D

Common features:

- AR is often immaterial; cash runway is central.
- CFO is usually negative due to R&D burn.
- Financing need, shelf registration, ATM program, debt covenants, and trial milestones matter.
- Revenue may be collaboration or license revenue, not recurring commercial sales.

Interpretation adjustments:

- Do not overemphasize AR if revenue is minimal.
- Negative CFO is expected, but runway and funding access determine risk.
- Current ratio can be high due to cash and marketable securities, but burn rate matters.
- Near-term debt or milestone obligations can be critical even if current liabilities look manageable.

Follow-up focus:

- Cash and marketable securities runway based on recent burn.
- R&D and G&A cash burn trend.
- Trial milestones, financing plans, shelf/ATM availability.
- Debt covenants, royalty obligations, and collaboration commitments.

## Energy / Commodity

Common features:

- Working capital and cash flow can move with commodity prices.
- Inventory and receivables may reflect price changes rather than only volume.
- Capex and debt service can be high.
- Hedging, reserve base, and borrowing-base facilities may matter.

Interpretation adjustments:

- CFO volatility must be read with commodity price and hedging context.
- Current liquidity can depend on borrowing-base redeterminations.
- Debt maturity risk should include revolver borrowing base, hedges, and capex flexibility.
- Inventory value can move with market prices.

Follow-up focus:

- Hedging book and realized prices.
- Borrowing-base availability and redetermination dates.
- Maintenance vs growth capex flexibility.
- Debt maturities and reserve-based lending covenants.

## Highly Levered / Refinancing-Sensitive Company

Common features:

- Total debt, maturity wall, interest cost, covenant headroom, and market access dominate.
- Cash flow can be adequate in normal periods but fragile if refinancing markets tighten.
- Current debt classification changes can materially alter liquidity view.

Interpretation adjustments:

- Strong current ratio does not offset large near-term maturities unless liquidity sources are committed and available.
- Debt reclassification from current to non-current needs note review.
- Positive CFO is a mitigant, but compare against interest expense, capex, and maturities.
- Missing covenant or maturity detail should cap confidence.

Follow-up focus:

- Maturity schedule by year.
- Revolver availability, covenant headroom, and borrowing restrictions.
- Interest rate exposure and hedges.
- Refinancing plans, amendments, waivers, and subsequent events.

## Consumer Durable / Auto / Dealer-Linked Business

Common features:

- Demand cyclicality, inventory/channel inventory, financing receivables, and dealer health can matter.
- Captive finance or receivable financing can blur operating and credit risk.
- Rising rates can pressure customer financing and demand.

Interpretation adjustments:

- AR and finance receivables should be separated when disclosed.
- Inventory growth can indicate channel pressure if demand weakens.
- Bad debt risk may sit in finance receivables rather than trade AR.
- Liquidity should consider floorplan, securitization, and credit facility availability.

Follow-up focus:

- Inventory by channel and dealer inventory levels.
- Finance receivable delinquencies, charge-offs, and allowance.
- Securitization / floorplan facilities.
- Demand trend and promotional financing.

## When Firm Type Is Unclear

If firm type is unclear:

- Do not force an overlay.
- Use the core six-module framework.
- Identify which business model details are missing.
- Add a follow-up question asking for segment revenue model, customer type, billing cycle, and operating cycle.
