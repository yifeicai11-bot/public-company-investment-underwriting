# Risk Rating Boundary Guidance

Use this file when assigning Low / Medium / High module ratings or an overall public-company credit and liquidity view.

This is not a mechanical scorecard. Ratings should reflect filing evidence, trend direction, materiality, plausible benign explanations, plausible adverse explanations, and confidence.

## Rating Philosophy

Build the rating from evidence, not from company reputation or the reason the company was selected.

Use this sequence:

1. Extract the fact.
2. Compare against prior period and scale.
3. Decide whether the fact is material to short-term credit or liquidity.
4. Consider benign explanations.
5. Consider adverse explanations.
6. Assign risk level and confidence.

Do not force all modules to have the same rating. A company can have Low receivables risk and High refinancing risk, or High working-capital pressure but Low near-term debt pressure.

## Overall Rating Boundaries

### Low Overall Risk

Use Low only when most of the following are true:

- No module is High.
- At most one module is Medium, and it is clearly mitigated.
- Current liquidity is supported by cash, short-term investments, operating cash flow, available credit facilities, or limited near-term obligations.
- Operating cash flow is positive or temporary weakness is well explained.
- Debt maturities and covenant issues do not appear material over the next 12-24 months.
- Public data is sufficient to support the conclusion.

### Medium Overall Risk

Use Medium when any of the following are true:

- Two or more modules are Medium.
- One module is High but is offset by strong liquidity, cash flow, or limited debt maturity exposure.
- The company has material AR, working-capital, debt, or cash-flow pressure that requires monitoring but does not clearly indicate near-term stress.
- Public filings support the direction of risk but leave important details missing, such as receivable aging, revolver capacity, or covenant headroom.

### High Overall Risk

Use High when any of the following are true:

- Short-term obligations appear to exceed reliable liquidity without a clear mitigating source.
- Operating cash flow is negative or deteriorating while liquidity is weakening.
- Large near-term maturities, covenant pressure, waiver language, default language, or going-concern language is present.
- Receivables, inventory, or working capital show severe deterioration and cash generation is weak.
- Public disclosures indicate dependence on refinancing, capital raising, asset sales, or amendments to meet near-term obligations.

## Cross-Module Override Signals

These signals should normally prevent a Low overall rating:

- Current ratio below 1.0x with low cash coverage and weak operating cash flow.
- Cash or liquidity declines while current liabilities or current debt rise.
- Current debt or maturities within 12 months materially exceed cash plus short-term investments.
- Positive earnings but negative operating cash flow caused by AR or inventory build.
- AR and inventory both increase materially while revenue or cash flow weakens.
- Debt is high relative to cash and CFO, and interest expense is rising.
- Filing includes going-concern, default, covenant waiver, or liquidity-stress language.

These signals should normally prevent a High overall rating unless other evidence supports it:

- Strong cash plus short-term investments relative to current liabilities.
- Consistently positive operating cash flow and free cash flow.
- No material current debt or near-term maturity wall.
- AR and inventory pressure is seasonal, reversed after period-end, or clearly explained.
- Company has disclosed substantial unused revolver availability with no covenant pressure.

## Module Rating Boundaries

### 1. Receivables Quality

Low:

- AR is small relative to revenue or stable as a percentage of revenue.
- AR growth is broadly in line with revenue growth.
- DSO, aging, or collection commentary is stable when disclosed.
- No material customer concentration, extended terms, factoring, or receivable sale concerns are disclosed.

Medium:

- AR is material to liquidity, commonly above about 35% of quarterly revenue, or AR growth exceeds revenue growth.
- AR is stable but aging, concentration, or collection detail is missing.
- Contract assets, unbilled receivables, or long payment cycles are material.
- The issue may be normal for the business model, but it is large enough to monitor.

High:

- AR is very large relative to revenue, commonly above about 75-100% of quarterly revenue, and rising.
- AR growth materially exceeds revenue growth without a clear explanation.
- Aging deteriorates, past-due balances rise, or collections are delayed.
- Customer concentration, extended terms, factoring with recourse, or disputed receivables create material collection risk.

### 2. Bad Debt / Credit Loss Risk

Low:

- Allowance, provisions, write-offs, and reserve methodology appear stable.
- Allowance coverage is consistent with receivable quality and customer profile.
- AR exposure is immaterial even if reserve detail is limited.

Medium:

- Allowance ratio declines while AR grows or collection risk rises.
- Provisions or write-offs increase but remain manageable.
- Allowance detail, write-offs, or aging is not sufficiently disclosed for a clean conclusion.
- Customer credit quality or macro conditions have worsened, but filings do not show severe losses.

High:

- Write-offs, provisions, or credit losses rise materially.
- Allowance coverage falls despite worsening AR, aging, or customer risk.
- Management discloses deteriorating customer credit quality, disputes, concessions, or collection issues.
- Reserve releases improve earnings while receivable quality worsens.

### 3. Short-Term Liquidity

Low:

- Current ratio is comfortably above 1.0x, often above about 1.5x, and liquidity quality is strong.
- Cash plus short-term investments, operating cash flow, and/or disclosed revolver availability support near-term obligations.
- Restricted cash is not a major part of visible liquidity.
- No material short-term debt pressure is visible.

Medium:

- Current ratio is near 1.0-1.5x, or cash-only coverage is modest.
- Liquidity depends on continued CFO, receivable collections, inventory conversion, or revolver access.
- Cash declines, but current liabilities or debt do not create immediate stress.
- Liquidity appears adequate but important details are missing.

High:

- Current ratio is below 1.0x and cash coverage is weak.
- Current debt, maturities, or payables exceed reliable liquidity.
- Restricted cash limits available cash.
- Management discloses liquidity stress, going-concern uncertainty, dependence on financing, or inability to fund operations without external support.

### 4. Cash Flow Conversion

Low:

- CFO is positive and broadly in line with or above net income.
- Free cash flow is positive or temporary capex explains the difference.
- Working-capital movements do not repeatedly consume cash.

Medium:

- CFO is weak or negative for one period, but annual CFO is positive or management gives a plausible timing explanation.
- Net income is positive but CFO is below earnings due to working-capital movements.
- Free cash flow is negative but driven by identifiable growth capex or temporary timing.

High:

- Positive earnings repeatedly fail to convert into CFO.
- CFO is negative while cash, liquidity, or credit metrics deteriorate.
- Free cash flow is negative due to operating weakness rather than only investment timing.
- AR, inventory, or contract asset build explains cash burn.

### 5. Working Capital Pressure

Low:

- AR, inventory, and payables move broadly with revenue and operating cycle.
- Working capital is stable or supports cash generation.
- No signs of stretched suppliers, inventory build, or collection delay.

Medium:

- AR or inventory rises faster than revenue for one period.
- Payables rise materially and may be supporting cash.
- Working capital consumes cash but could reflect seasonality, growth, or project timing.
- Data is insufficient to decide whether pressure is temporary.

High:

- AR and inventory both rise while revenue or cash flow weakens.
- Payables are stretched or supplier obligations rise sharply.
- Working capital consumes cash across multiple periods.
- Inventory obsolescence, markdown, slow-moving stock, disputed receivables, or delayed collections are disclosed.

### 6. Near-Term Debt / Refinancing Pressure

Low:

- Current debt and 12-24 month maturities are small relative to liquidity.
- Interest expense is manageable relative to CFO or EBITDA proxy.
- Company has no visible covenant pressure and has disclosed adequate credit availability.

Medium:

- Total debt is high relative to cash, but near-term maturities appear manageable.
- Current debt is material, but cash flow or credit facilities appear sufficient.
- Interest expense is rising but not yet destabilizing.
- Maturity schedule, covenant headroom, or revolver availability is missing and should be checked.

High:

- Large maturities within 12-24 months exceed visible liquidity.
- Company relies on refinancing, amendments, waivers, asset sales, or capital raising.
- Covenant breach, default, waiver, or going-concern language appears.
- Interest expense rises while CFO weakens and liquidity declines.

## Confidence Rules

High confidence:

- Direct filing data supports the conclusion across at least two periods.
- Relevant note disclosure confirms the interpretation.
- The metric and trend are clear, and no major counter-evidence is missing.

Medium confidence:

- Data supports the direction, but one important detail is missing.
- XBRL metrics are available but filing-note detail has not been reviewed.
- The signal may be explained by industry norms, seasonality, or timing.

Low confidence:

- Public disclosure is limited or inconsistent.
- Key metrics are missing.
- Conclusion depends on indirect evidence, news, or partial data.

Do not pair a strong conclusion with Low confidence. If the data gap is important, state the gap directly and make the rating provisional.

## Rating Language

Use:

- "appears" when based on public filings and not confirmed by management.
- "requires monitoring" for Medium signals with plausible benign explanations.
- "is the primary risk driver" only when evidence clearly shows materiality.
- "not visible from public filings" when a data point is missing.

Avoid:

- Formal credit-rating language.
- Buy/sell/hold conclusions.
- Distress conclusions based on one metric alone.
- Reputational assumptions about well-known companies.
